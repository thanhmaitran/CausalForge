CausalForge Research Report

Research methodology, experimental results and discussion

CausalForge: A Reproducible Platform for Causal Effect Estimation, Heterogeneous Treatment Analysis and Robust Decision Intelligence

Abstract

We present CausalForge, a from-scratch Python platform for ATE/ATT/CATE estimation, sensitivity analysis and budget-constrained policy evaluation, and a benchmark on synthetic structural causal models with exact ground truth. In controlled experiments, adjustment estimators remove observed confounding bias (|bias| ≤ 0.007 for AIPW versus up to 1.07 for the naive contrast), but all fail under hidden confounding (AIPW bias up to 1.62, CI coverage 0%). Robustness values and permutation placebo tests did not detect hidden confounding, whereas a valid negative-control outcome did. An honest causal forest achieved lower √PEHE (0.17–0.18) than X-learner (0.33) and T-learner (0.47–0.54) on a nonlinear simulator, but its heuristic pointwise intervals covered only 67–69%. Real-data benchmarks (IHDP, ACIC 2016, Criteo, LaLonde) are implemented but not yet executed; their results are reported in §6.2 once run.

1. Introduction

Research question: how reliably can interventions' causal effects be estimated from observational and experimental data while quantifying uncertainty, heterogeneity and robustness? RQ1–RQ5 follow the project specification (estimator accuracy, heterogeneity recovery, confounding, sensitivity, policy construction).

2. Related work

Potential outcomes (Rubin); structural causal models and the backdoor criterion (Pearl); propensity scores (Rosenbaum & Rubin, 1983); doubly robust estimation; meta-learners (Künzel et al., 2019); double/debiased ML (Chernozhukov et al., 2018); causal forests (Wager & Athey, 2018; Athey, Tibshirani & Wager, 2019); omitted-variable-bias sensitivity (Cinelli & Hazlett, 2020); policy learning. The original papers should be read and cited in full before submission; this draft lists them as pointers and does not paraphrase their results.

3. Background

Estimands: ITE, ATE, CATE, ATT. Identification assumptions: consistency, conditional exchangeability, positivity, SUTVA. A numerically accurate estimator does not imply identification.

4. Methodology

Implemented estimators and diagnostics are listed in the technical report (§6–§8). Ground truth in simulation is computed from structural equations. Policy value is exact in simulation and IPW-estimated in randomized data.

5. Experimental design

Synthetic SCMs (linear/nonlinear, heterogeneous, randomized/observational, hidden confounder), synthetic panels (DiD) and instruments (IV). Sample sizes, replications and seeds are in artifacts/results/*.csv; the full suite is scripts/run_experiments.py (≈2 minutes, one CPU core).

6. Results

6.1 Synthetic benchmarks (measured)

  • Confounding (EXP-05). Naive bias 0.35 / 0.63 / 0.94 / 1.07 at strengths 0.5 / 1 / 2 / 3; AIPW |bias| ≤ 0.007.
  • Sample size (EXP-07). AIPW RMSE 0.118, 0.069, 0.035, 0.024, 0.010 at n = 500, 1k, 5k, 10k, 50k (≈1/√n).
  • Meta-learners (EXP-02), √PEHE. Linear: S 0.252, X 0.325, T 0.487. Nonlinear: S 0.289, X 0.326, T 0.537.
  • DML vs AIPW (EXP-03), 30 reps. Coverage 0.90–0.97 (Monte-Carlo error ≈ ±0.05); RMSE 0.04–0.07.
  • Honest forest (EXP-03b), 20 reps. √PEHE 0.166 (randomized) and 0.180 (observational) versus X-learner 0.331/0.329 and T-learner 0.472/0.536; pointwise coverage 0.690/0.665.
  • Hidden confounding (EXP-06). AIPW (observed X) bias 0.23 / 0.83 / 1.62 at U-strength 0.5 / 1.0 / 1.5, coverage 3% / 0% / 0%; oracle AIPW with U unbiased. Mean RV rose 0.36 → 0.52. Permutation placebo rejected 5.5% / 3.5% (strength 0 / 1.5).
  • Negative control outcome (EXP-09). Rejection 12% at no hidden confounding (nominal 5%, 50 reps) and 100% at strengths 0.5 and 1.0. The NCO here is valid by construction (shares U, unaffected by T).
  • DiD (EXP-10). Parallel trends: bias −0.004, coverage 0.97, pre-trend rejection 2%. Violated trends: bias 0.90, coverage 0%, pre-trend rejection 100%.
  • IV (EXP-11). OLS bias 0.33. Strong valid IV (F≈505): bias −0.001, coverage 1.00. Weak IV (F≈4.3): RMSE 2.18. Exclusion violated: bias 0.30 (F is large, so F cannot diagnose this).
  • Policy (EXP-08). Learned top-k policies approach the oracle and beat random at budgets 0.1–0.7; IPW policy value 0.945 vs exact 0.943.

7. Discussion

Adjustment fixes only measured confounding. Diagnostics differ in what they can see: RV quantifies fragility but rises with a biased estimate; placebo tests are blind to confounding that does not distort the permuted contrast; negative controls and pre-trend tests detect specific violations when their own assumptions hold; first-stage F detects weak instruments but not exclusion violations. S-learner's advantage in this simulator reflects smooth heterogeneity and shrinkage toward a constant effect; it should not be generalised.

8. Limitations

Low-dimensional well-specified simulators; heuristic forest intervals; PSM without SE; IPW SE conservative; DML-PLR weighted target; real-data and reference-library cross-checks outstanding.

9. Conclusion

CausalForge provides a tested, reproducible implementation of estimation, heterogeneity, sensitivity and policy tools, and quantifies where each tool fails. Contributions: unified framework, DAG/adjustment engine, reproducible multi-DGP benchmark, sensitivity-method comparison, uncertainty-aware policy evaluation (on simulations so far).
